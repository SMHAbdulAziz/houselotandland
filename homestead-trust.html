    What it does

    What a homestead trust can and can't do

    A revocable living trust is a simple way to plan who gets your home and who manages it if you can't. Here's an honest picture.

    It can

    • Pass your home to your beneficiaries without probate for the house
    • Let a successor trustee you choose manage or sell the home if you become unable to
    • Keep your Texas homestead exemption, when the trust meets Tax Code §11.13(j) (ours is written to)
    • Be changed or revoked by you at any time

    It can't

    • Protect your home from creditors beyond what Texas homestead law already gives you
    • Lower your property taxes or remove your mortgage
    • Replace a will for everything else you own
    • Replace advice from an attorney about your own situation

    Questions

    Homestead trust FAQ

    Is this legal advice?

    No. This is self-help document software. You answer the questions and the software fills in Texas templates. We are not a law firm and can't tell you whether a trust is right for you. If you have questions about your situation, talk to a Texas attorney.

    Will I keep my homestead exemption?

    Texas lets a home held in a "qualifying trust" keep the residence homestead exemption (Tax Code §11.13(j)). The trust must give you the right to live in the home rent free for life or until the trust is revoked, and the deed into the trust must be recorded. Our trust is written to meet those requirements. Some appraisal districts ask you to re-file Form 50-114 after the transfer.

    What about my mortgage?

    Federal law (the Garn-St Germain Act) generally keeps a lender from calling a home loan due because you transfer your home into your own living trust, when you stay a beneficiary and keep living there. It's still wise to let your lender and insurer know.

    Can I change it later?

    Yes. The trust is revocable. You can amend or revoke it any time while you're living.

    What do I do after I print the documents?

    Sign the trust and certification before a notary, sign the deed before a notary, and record the deed with your county clerk. Your package includes step-by-step instructions, or you can add recording and we'll file it for you.

    Do I still need a will?

    Usually yes. The trust covers the home you put in it. A will covers everything else, including anything you forget to put in the trust.
